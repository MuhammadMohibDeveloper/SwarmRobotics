Robofest 2026 · Swarm concept: the binary bridge experiment in ants

Not every path to opportunity is equal.

What if collective intelligence could find the most inclusive one?

BridgeForward releases a colony of 40 simulated ants between a person and an opportunity. Two routes lead there. Cost, safety, gender equality, disability access, digital access and language decide which trail the colony reinforces. When a barrier appears, the colony changes its route.

    Fig. 02 · How it learns
    
        
    Explore, evaluate, reinforce, adapt.
    
        
    In the binary bridge experiment, ants offered two bridges to food soon concentrate on one. No ant knows which bridge is better. Each one follows the pheromone left by the ants before it and adds its own. BridgeForward uses the same feedback loop, but the trail is reinforced in proportion to how inclusive each route is.
    
      
    
      
      
        
    1. 
          01
          
          
      Explore
      
          
      Each step, a wave of 8 agents leaves the start. Each one picks a path using the current selection probability. About 5% ignore the trail and choose at random, so the colony keeps testing the alternative.
      
        
      2. 
        
    2. 
          02
          
          
      Evaluate
      
          
      Six inclusion factors (0 = severe barrier, 100 = fully inclusive) are weighted into one score. If any factor falls below 35, a weakest-link penalty applies, so one excluding barrier cannot be averaged away.
      
        
      3. 
        
    3. 
          03
          
          
      Reinforce
      
          
      Every agent that walks a path lays pheromone in proportion to that path's inclusion score. A better path gets more visits and more pheromone per visit, so the feedback loop builds a collective preference.
      
        
      4. 
        
    4. 
          04
          
          
      Adapt
      
          
      Each step, every trail keeps 92% of its pheromone and the rest evaporates. When a barrier appears, the affected path loses score straight away and its trail fades over the following steps, so the colony changes route gradually.
      
        
      5. 
      
    
      
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Why it cannot lock in
    
          
    Pheromone is bounded to a minimum and maximum, a MAX–MIN Ant System technique, so neither trail can disappear or grow without limit. Evaporation erodes old decisions and the exploration rate keeps sampling the other route. A path that becomes exclusionary therefore loses the colony, and a path that improves can win it back.
    
          
    The run uses a seeded random number generator, so the guided demo produces the same sequence every time. Manual runs still respond to every change you make.

    Fig. 03 · Why inclusion matters
    
        
    A route can be open on paper and closed in practice.
    
        
    Someone may technically be eligible for an opportunity and still be kept out by its cost, an unsafe journey, a rule that disadvantages their gender, an inaccessible building, a missing device or a language they don't speak. BridgeForward makes each of these barriers a measurable factor, so they change the route the colony chooses.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    
          
    5UN Sustainable Development Goal
    
          
    Gender Equality
    
          
    Gender is part of the calculation, not decoration. A gender-biased eligibility rule lowers the path's gender-equality factor. That lowers its inclusion score, then the pheromone it receives, then the chance that future agents choose it.
    
          
    “End all forms of discrimination against all women and girls everywhere.”SDG Target 5.1
    
          
      
            
    • Gender equality factor. Models eligibility rules or practices that disadvantage people based on gender.
      • 
            
    • Travel safety. Unsafe journeys, such as travelling after dark, can affect women disproportionately. The “Woman commuting after dark” profile gives safety and gender more weight.
      • 
            
    • Digital access. Relates to Target 5.b on using enabling technology to promote women's empowerment.
      • 
          
    
        
    
        
    
          
    10UN Sustainable Development Goal
    
          
    Reduced Inequalities
    
          
    Inequality is modelled as a dynamic system rather than a static chart. Cost, disability access, digital access, language and discriminatory eligibility each shift which route the collective considers best. Removing one barrier can change the outcome.
    
          
    “Ensure equal opportunity and reduce inequalities of outcome, including by eliminating discriminatory laws, policies and practices…”SDG Target 10.3
    
          
      
            
    • Cost / affordability. Fees and transport costs that price people out.
      • 
            
    • Disability access. Step-free routes, accessible buildings and services (Target 10.2: inclusion “irrespective of … disability”).
      • 
            
    • Language access. Whether help is available in the languages people speak.
      • 
            
    • Digital access. Online-only applications exclude people without devices or connectivity.
      •

    Fig. 04 · Competition documentation
    
        
    BridgeForward, documented.
    
        
    BridgeForward: Ant-Inspired Pathways to Opportunity. Swarm concept: the binary bridge experiment in ants. Tagline: When barriers change, the path should change too.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    01
    Problem
    People may technically have access to an opportunity while practical inequality prevents meaningful access. Cost, safety, gender discrimination, disability access, digital access and language decide whether a route is actually usable.
    
        
    02
    Proposed solution
    BridgeForward models two possible routes to an opportunity. A colony of agents uses collective reinforcement inspired by ant colony optimisation to discover which route currently gives the most inclusive outcome, and re-routes when conditions change.
    
        
    03
    SDG 5 connection
    Gender-based barriers are explicit, measurable pathway factors. A gender-biased eligibility rule lowers the inclusion score, reinforcement and future selection probability, so it changes the outcome as well as the explanation text.
    
        
    04
    SDG 10 connection
    The system models inequalities in cost, disability access, digital access, language, safety and discriminatory eligibility. Traveller profiles show that the most inclusive route depends on who is travelling.
    
        
    05
    Technology
    • HTML5, CSS3 and vanilla JavaScript in a single file
    • Three.js (r149) for the procedural 3D bridge, with an SVG fallback
    • An algorithm inspired by ant colony optimisation and MAX–MIN Ant System
    • Canvas 2D live chart, responsive and accessible UI
    
        
    06
    Innovation
    Inequality is shown as a dynamic system instead of a static chart. Changing one barrier can change collective behaviour, and every decision is explained from the live numbers.
    
        
    07
    Impact
    The concept could eventually support decision-making tools that compare routes to education, healthcare, jobs, funding or public services using real accessibility and inclusion data.
    
        
    08
    Limitations
    This competition application is a simulation prototype, not a real-world decision authority. Scores are illustrative inputs, not measurements, and the routes are fictional.
