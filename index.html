01 — The measurement

Twenty years of
the same four questions.

Since 2006 the same four dimensions have been measured the same way, in the same economies: economic participation, education, health, and political power. That consistency is what makes the gap arguable with evidence rather than anecdote.

The 2026 reading is the twentieth. Progress is real. It is also slower than a human lifetime.

Of the global gender gap is closed

Up 0.4 points on 2025, across 148 economies — the fastest single-year gain since the pandemic, and still a third of the distance unwalked.

Until full parity at today’s pace

Measured across the twenty-year cohort of economies. A child born the year of this report would not see parity, nor would their grandchild.

03 — The instruments

Four tools, one
principle: measure, then move.

A measurement nobody acts on is decoration. Plumbline is built as four connected instruments — each one turns a number on this page into something a person, a team, or an employer can actually do this week.

01

Gap Index

Answer six questions about your country, sector and role, and get your own position against the published parity benchmarks — with the source behind every comparison, so you can take it into a salary review.

60 seconds · no account
02

Mentor Match

Pairs people with mentors already inside the rooms they are trying to enter, matched on sector and constraint rather than on seniority alone. Six structured sessions, then a warm introduction.

6 sessions
03

Skills Ladder

Short, verifiable credentials mapped to the roles where the gap is widest, each one signed so it travels across borders and employers without being re-explained.

Portable · verifiable
04

Open Ledger

Employers publish their own pay and promotion gaps in a single comparable format. Transparency is the one intervention that cannot be undone quietly — once a number is public, it has to be defended or fixed.

For organisations

Plumbline is a competition concept, not a live service. The four instruments describe what the platform would do; the data and the sources behind it are real and current.
